Women in Design

Throughout history, women have been restricted from many different aspects of society. Laws and social barriers prevented them from “contributing” to society outside the home.

Despite the barriers placed before them, they have persevered. Even though they faced restrictions, women have always been involved in design, often without recognition.

Our goal with this exhibit is to highlight the impact women have had on the design world through many different styles, including architecture, landscape design, fashion, and much more.

This exhibit is a collaborative effort by our Honors Colloquium 390 class, “Women in Design.” This website consolidates all the research collected throughout the semester to highlight women’s work throughout history and their impact on design. We have highlighted important historical information, women in design throughout the years, and many artifacts that caught our attention. Through this exhibit, we want to emphasize that women have always been involved in design, regardless of the barriers in place, and they will continue to be involved, no matter what comes next.

History and Social Reform

1977

All states pass laws to take away women’s right to vote.

1800s

First Wave of Feminism begins, seeking “equality within patriarchal social constructs”.

1831

The National Academy of Design in New York becomes one of the first major institutions to let women study in basic art classes.

1848

Seneca Falls Convention, New York. The Declaration of Sentiments is signed, a plea to end discrimination against women in all social spheres.

1855

Establishment of the Society of Female Artists.

1881

Louise Bethune became the first female architect to open her own firm.

1890

Wyoming is the first state to grant women the right to vote in all elections.

1897

Integration of women in the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris.

1900

All states have legislation to grant married women some control over their property and earnings–modeled after the NY Married Women’s Property Act (1848).

1920

The 19th Amendment to the U.S. Constitution is ratified, granting all white women the right to vote.

1938

The Fair Labor Standards Act establishes minimum wage without regard to sex.

1959

Norma Merrick Sklarek became the first African American woman member of the AIA.

1960s

Second Wave Feminism begins, advocating for “acknowledgement of difference.” Beginning to question other things, such as the built environment and spatial concerns, to try and dismiss inequality and rather embrace differences.

1962

Norma Merrick Sklarek became the first African American woman to obtain an architecture license in California.

1963

The Equal Pay Act passes, promising equal wages for the same work, regardless of race, religion, nationality, or sex of the worker.

1964

Title VII of the Civil Rights Act passes, prohibiting employment discrimination based on race, religion, nationality, or sex.

1965

The Voting Rights Act gave BIPOC women the right to an equal chance to vote.

1965

Weeks v. Southern Bell marks a triumph against restrictive labor laws and company regulations on hours and conditions of women’s work, opening up opportunities that were previously only for men.

1968

Executive Order 11246 prohibits sex discrimination by government contractors and requires affirmative action plans for hiring women.

1971

Reed v. Reed The U.S. Supreme Court ruled it unconstitutional to hold automatic preference for males as administrators of wills. This is the first time the Court disallows a law treating men and women differently. The Supreme Court officially declares women as “persons”.

1974

Cleveland Board of Education v. LaFleur determines it is illegal to force a pregnant employee to take maternity leave on the assumption they are incapable of working.

1974

The Women’s Educational Equity Act (drafted by Arlene Horowitz) funds the development of non-sexist teaching materials, and model programs to encourage full educational opportunities for women and girls.

1981

Kirchberg v. Feenstra overturns state laws designating a husband as the “head and master” with unilateral control of property jointly owned with his wife.

1990s

Emergence of Ecofeminism, shifting the women’s role to outside of the home.
